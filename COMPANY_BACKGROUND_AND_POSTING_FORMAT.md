# Shared company background and posting presentation

All job details, whether previously ingested or newly fetched, use `renderDescription` and `JobDetailContent`. Employer templates do not choose a separate presentation. Inter is self-hosted throughout the site, including controls, numbers, and italic text; the main Latin font is preloaded.

Summary and Full Posting crossfade while their shared container animates its height. The selected toggle indicator slides between views. Rapid changes cancel the previous animation, changing jobs resets the body, and reduced-motion preferences disable the content animation. The outgoing copy is hidden from assistive technology and cannot receive input.

Browsing cards retain the logo, job title, and company. Their metadata contains only location, employer-provided salary when available, and posting age. Roles, workplace type, academic term, and skills remain in job details and filters. Country aliases are deduplicated using the source country, so `Toronto, ON, CA, Canada` becomes `Toronto, ON, Canada`, while `San Francisco, CA, USA` retains California. Non-salary equity and bonus details stay in the detail pane.

The detail view defaults to a concise Summary with consistent Requirements, Responsibilities, Preferred qualifications, and Role details headings. It recognizes short labels and long ATS question labels, including bold-only SuccessFactors headings. The introduction has its own limit; requirements and responsibilities each get up to four complete source blocks, with balanced selection inside a 1,500-character budget. Labelled requisition IDs and generic recruitment introductions are omitted from Summary. Full Posting shows all sanitized employer text, including original labels, benefits, company introductions, and disclosures. The language choice applies to both views; changing jobs resets to Summary. Summary omits additional detail without generating claims or cutting a qualification halfway through.

The toolbar offers five native selects: combined location/country, job type, experience, workplace, and date posted. Search handles company and skill queries. Existing URLs with multiple choices or removed filters retain visible removable chips. Search shares the job-list column on desktop, with filters immediately to its right and a 12px gap. The page is capped at 1440px; large desktops retain a 600px search/list column and full-size filter controls. The closed controls use soft borders and clear active states, while the native browser/OS pickers retain their keyboard behavior and system light/dark colors. The page and original company logos stay light.

A desktop visit without a selected-job URL automatically selects the first result after the current query resolves, replacing the current history entry. Filters and pagination select the first new result; stale cards, empty/error results, and hidden tabs do not trigger selection. Explicit job URLs are preserved. Desktop detail stays open without a close icon; phones keep the dismissible overlay. Apply retains its official URL as a text link with a left-to-right underline on hover or keyboard focus, with reduced-motion support. Company links omit YouTube in every shared view.

The bottom branding footer is removed. The main location and additional-location toggle share one row when space permits; expanding shows only additional locations, without repeating the main city. Employer street addresses appear immediately with readable capitalization, preserved unit/postal codes, and a map-search link. An exact trailing city match is omitted from the address label because it already appears above; the full address is retained in the map destination and original posting. Remote roles identify the address as employer-listed. Optional application links create a header row only when present, and the header/facts divider has compact spacing on desktop and phones.

Company logos use the same rounded square component in cards, details, company profiles, and the directory. Card logos align with the first title line, regardless of wrapped titles, metadata, or skills. Detail headers keep company links beside the name, with a compact one-pixel gap before the business label. The logo component first requests the verified company domain’s favicon from Google’s favicon service at 128px, then tries the existing API logo and stored brand asset, and finally shows a generic organization icon. A new employer’s explicit official website can supply the favicon domain; job-board domains and guessed domains are excluded. Artwork keeps its original colors and aspect ratio, and failed sources do not loop or carry into another employer’s logo.

- Explicit pay, hours, work-location, business-area, deadline, address, and job-family fields are lifted into metadata. Conflicting or ambiguous values are preserved rather than guessed.
- Pay explanations remain accessible under Compensation details. The sanitized original posting remains available in full.
- Company background is independent of the job description. The company-domain catalog covers all 112 mapped domains, including all 79 currently configured employers and their aliases. Existing sourced company facts are retained; the new descriptions add no headcounts, dates, or benefits.
- Future postings automatically reuse their company profile. Previously unknown companies with an official website can load its public descriptive metadata through `/company-background`. Successful results are cached. Private destinations, credential-bearing URLs, and redirects to another company are rejected.
- There is no missing-background diagnostic in the job UI. When a new employer has no accessible descriptive source, its company identity and available links remain; the application does not manufacture a company description.

`company-backgrounds.test.tsx` checks every entry in `config/target_companies.json` and every mapped alias. Adding a configured employer without reusable company background fails that coverage check before publication. Posting-normalization tests exercise Workday heading blocks, inline bold labels, wrapper blocks, conflicting values, and the TD example.
